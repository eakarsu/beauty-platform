  5. How do you handle disagreements between business and technical stakeholders?

  > My approach is to start by understanding the outcome each side wants and the constraint each side is concerned about. Then I
  > try to make the tradeoff concrete.
  >
  > For a prediction project, for example, I would ask which mistakes are more costly: prioritizing a lead that does not convert,
  > or overlooking one that would have converted. That discussion helps connect technical evaluation with business priorities.
  >
  > Where possible, I would propose a small test with agreed success criteria. I would document the decision, the owner, and what

  This is an approach answer. If asked for a past disagreement, add a real example rather than presenting this hypothetical as an

  6. How do you leave customers able to operate a solution independently?

  > I would plan for handover throughout the implementation. That means identifying who owns the system, involving that team
  > early, and explaining how the solution behaves when something goes wrong.
  >
  > My handover would cover normal operation, monitoring, common failures, access requirements, and escalation procedures. For an
  > AI solution, I would also explain the model’s limitations and when a person should review its output.
  >
  > I would check readiness by asking the customer team to perform common tasks and work through a failure scenario themselves.
  > That gives stronger evidence of independence than simply delivering documentation.
